
INTEGRATED TRANSPORT MASTERPLAN FOR PŁOCK


Concept for the elimination of critical systemic risks and the development of logistical resilience in the feedstock heart of the Republic of Poland


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY


Concept for the elimination of the "single point of failure" (SPoF) risk in the feedstock heart of the Republic of Poland

The subject of this study is to demonstrate the urgent need for a comprehensive reconfiguration of the transport infrastructure of Płock – an agglomeration that constitutes the absolute foundation and geostrategic keystone of the fuel, energy, and defense security of the Republic of Poland. The current transport network generates critical risks to the continuity of state functions due to the lack of grade-separated connections between critical infrastructure and the express road network, as well as the inadequacy of the railway network for mass cargo transport and passenger services.
The Płock agglomeration concentrates the country's key critical infrastructure (ORLEN S.A. and PERN S.A.), while remaining a transport island without adequate road and rail connections. In the face of hybrid threats, the current layout generates a critical "single point of failure" (SPoF) risk.
Achieving full logistical and economic resilience of this key industrial hub requires the parallel implementation of an integrated package of infrastructural solutions, based on the pillars listed below.

INTEGRATED INVESTMENT PACKAGE (MASTERPLAN FOR PŁOCK):

• NATIONAL ROAD PILLAR: Express Road S60 (2x2): Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) with a new bridge (2x2) over the Vistula River in Rydzyno, compliant with military load classification MLC 150 / STANAG.
• LOCAL ROAD PILLAR: City Ring Road (2x2) with a new bridge (2x2) in Maszewo compliant with military load classification MLC 150 / STANAG for the segregation of ADR transport, an internal east-west city corridor (2x2), access roads (2x2) connecting the City Ring Road with express roads (the planned S10 and postulated S60), as well as single-carriageway logistics and transport roads serving the ORLEN industrial complex directly.
• RAILWAY FREIGHT PILLAR: Freight Bypass of Płock (double-track, electrified) routing RID transport away from residential areas with a new double-track bridge in the Maszewo region, supplemented by railway links integrating the bypass with planned routes (the CMK-North trunk line and the Płock – Ciechanów – Przasnysz railway line) and the existing railway line No. 33 (LK 33).
• PASSENGER-EMERGENCY RAILWAY PILLAR: Construction of a second track on the electrified section of LK 33 between Kutno and Trzepowo, a new double-track alignment of LK 33 in Płock on the Góry – Płock station section with a new double-track railway bridge in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge, as well as a grade-separated triangular junction on the border of the left-bank Płock districts of Radziwie and Góry, along with the relocation of the Płock Radziwie station and the commissioning of the new Płock Góry station.
• INTERMODAL COMPONENT – MULTIMODAL HUB: A river-rail-road port on the Włocławek Reservoir.
The investments presented in the Masterplan aim to break the transport peripherality of the region and fully integrate Płock into the national logistics system (the entire network of connections is shown in Figure 1).
The key element in the road component is the high-priority section: Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7). The postulated route is an integral part of a broader, macro-regional express road corridor along the Kalisz – Zambrów axis (Figure 2), the implementation of which will geometrically increase the transport resilience of central Poland.
In the railway component, the Masterplan emphasizes the construction of a second track and the full electrification of LK 33 on the Kutno – Trzepowo section, as a key link in the Railway Strategic Axis North – South (Zgierz – Kutno – Płock – Grudziądz – Laskowice Pomorskie), illustrated in Figure 3. In order to fully exploit the potential of this corridor, it is essential to take action at the governmental level to include the further modernization of the entire LK 33 north of the Trzepowo station (construction of a second track and electrification) and LK 208 on the Brodnica – Laskowice Pomorskie section in national investment plans. This action should become an element of the complementary plans of the national infrastructure manager (PKP Polskie Linie Kolejowe S.A.).
The condition for achieving a synergy effect is the systemic and continuous adaptation of the technical parameters of the public transport network to the dynamically changing feedstock and technological needs generated by strategic industrial plants in Płock.

PHASING STRATEGY (2027–2037):

• Phase I: Critical Priority (Years 2027–2030) – Bottleneck Elimination
	• Roads: Construction of the internal City Ring Road of Płock with a dual-carriageway (2x2) bridge in Maszewo (military load class MLC 150 / STANAG, ADR segregation), completion of the east-west corridor, commencement of access roads on the City Ring Road – express roads (S10 and S60) route, and logistics-transport corridors around the ORLEN production plant.
	• Rail: Construction of the double-track Freight Bypass of Płock along with a new freight-military bridge over the Vistula River in Maszewo and a link to LK 33, and the commissioning of the new Płock Góry station on LK 33.
• Phase II: Strategic Integration (Years 2030–2034) – Overcoming the Vistula Barrier and Connecting to the National Network
	• Roads: Construction of the strategic section of the S60 Express Road: Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) with a new dual-carriageway (2x2) bridge in Rydzyno (military load class MLC 150 / STANAG), and finalization of access roads on the City Ring Road – express roads route and logistics-transport corridors in the region of the ORLEN industrial complex.
	• Rail: Construction of a second track and electrification of LK 33 on the Kutno – Trzepowo route, a new passenger-emergency alignment of the line across the Vistula via a new bridge in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge with a grade-separated triangular junction on the border of Radziwie and Góry, and the Płock Radziwie station in its new location.
• Phase III: Full Synergy and Decarbonization (Years 2034–2037) – Multimodal Ring
	• Rail: Launch of the Płock Urban Fast Rail (SKM), construction of grade-separated crossings and links integrating the freight bypass with the CMK-North line and the route to Przasnysz.
	• Multimodal Hub: Construction of a river-rail-road port (Multimodal Hub) on the Włocławek Reservoir.
The timely implementation of the indicated implementation phases is the foundation for achieving multi-level benefits, crucial both for the development of the Płock region and for the stability of the entire country.

STRATEGIC BENEFITS FOR THE COUNTRY:

• FEEDSTOCK SECURITY: Full logistical redundancy for ORLEN and PERN (elimination of the risk of state paralysis).
• DEFENSE RESILIENCE (dual-use): Adaptation of transport corridors to NATO requirements (bridges compliant with military load class MLC 150 / STANAG), enabling rapid troop deployment along the eastern flank of the alliance.
• MODERN ECONOMY (high-tech, IT): Breaking transport isolation as an impulse to attract strategic investments from the high-tech sector, data centers, and Industry 4.0, powered by clean energy from the Płock energy transition hub.
• FINANCIAL BENEFITS (ROI): Billions in revenue to the state budget from customs duties and fuel taxes exceeding the investment outlays.
The estimated CapEx is PLN 17.4 – 21.8 billion for the years 2026–2036. Integrated investments in the Płock region meet the criteria for dual-use infrastructure, qualifying for immediate co-financing from the EU's CEF Transport – Military Mobility program and national defense funds.
The implementation of the integrated Masterplan for Płock is the key to building uncompromising resilience and full logistical redundancy in the region of the country's key strategic plants. The new axis of the S60 Express Road will become the central element of this system, permanently breaking the existing transport isolation of the region and guaranteeing secure freight distribution flows. The scale and interdisciplinary nature of the project require the urgent establishment of a dedicated interministerial team under the Council of Ministers, capable of coordinating the actions of government bodies, state-owned enterprises, and local governments. The implementation of these priority investments will eliminate the critical risk of paralysis of critical infrastructure, forming the foundation for the long-term energy, feedstock, and defense security of the Republic of Poland.

1. INTRODUCTION AND ESSENCE OF STRATEGIC ISSUES

Contemporary geopolitical challenges, escalating hybrid threats, and NATO military mobility requirements necessitate an immediate review of Poland's transport map in the region of its most important refining, petrochemical, and hydrogen hub – Płock. The paramount objective of the state must be to ensure the operational and defensive continuity of the Płock plants, which form the heart of the country's fuel supply. Despite such a fundamental role, years of systemic neglect have led to a situation where the city remains a transport island.
Hitherto isolated central plans – including the expansion of the S10 road, which bypasses the city far to the north, and routine modernizations of national routes to class GP standard – do not eliminate systemic risks. Such a layout completely deprives Płock of the chance to form even a partial orbital ring road. This represents a critical threat in crisis situations, drastically limiting the mobility of emergency services and preventing the efficient evacuation of the population from threatened zones of the agglomeration.
In this situation, a multi-vector approach is necessary. The structural reconfiguration of the railway network, the expansion of the integrated City Ring Road, and the introduction of the S60 Express Road as one of the main pillars of the national network are interdependent projects (Figure 1). These actions must be implemented through the coordinated cooperation of central ministries (Ministry of National Defense, Ministry of Infrastructure, Ministry of State Assets), local governments, and state-owned enterprises.
The presented document has the character of a framework, community-driven Masterplan. As a directional framework, it focuses on the strategic essence – the definition of transport corridors, risk mitigation, and systemic integration. Secondary detailed analyses have been deliberately omitted, leaving detailed traffic modeling and microscopic construction designs to the competence of the relevant local and government authorities.

2. SECURITY LANDSCAPE: STRATEGIC FUNCTION OF PŁOCK AND RISK IDENTIFICATION


2.1. Extraordinary Concentration of Critical Infrastructure and Requirement for Technical Flexibility

The Płock agglomeration concentrates a unique concentration in Europe of upper and lower-tier establishments with a high risk of major industrial accidents (in accordance with the SEVESO III directive). The ORLEN production complex and the PERN feedstock storage and logistics base are responsible for the continuity of fuel supplies for the economy and the Polish Armed Forces. This centralized critical infrastructure is currently in a logistical deadlock – the transport of refined products and feedstocks relies on conflicting, narrow single-carriageway roads and railway lines cutting through the middle of residential areas.
In this context, treating transport and feedstock infrastructure as static is a fundamental planning error. There is a critical necessity to legally and spatially sanction the principle of continuous, mutual adaptation of public transport corridors and underground pipeline networks. Contemporary engineering allows for the complete elimination of spatial conflicts – where projected roads and tracks closely approach existing pipeline corridors, industrial plants gain the ability to flexibly manage their infrastructure. By utilizing advanced trenchless methods (e.g., horizontal directional drilling HDD), transmission routes can be adaptively and seamlessly relocated in a manner that is completely non-invasive to newly established arteries. The technological coordination of these actions ensures that underground pipelines cease to be a planning barrier, becoming instead an element of an integrated logistics system. This same principle of spatial flexibility applies to overhead extra-high voltage power lines, which can be seamlessly integrated into new transport corridors through modern underground cabling techniques or by increasing pylon heights.
In summary: maintaining the transport status quo around the Płock industrial hub creates a critical systemic risk, directly undermining the foundations of civil security and the defense capabilities of the Republic of Poland. The rapid technological, feedstock, and production development of the refining and petrochemical complex, combined with an obsolete, inadequate, and conflicting transport network, will inevitably breach public safety thresholds. In the event of a major industrial accident, an act of sabotage, or an escalation of a hybrid conflict, the lack of segregated freight routes and independent strategic corridors will generate a catastrophic threat to human life, public health, and the urban structure of Płock. In the military dimension, maintaining the current state of infrastructure paralyzes the mobility of Allied NATO forces and the Polish Army (the military mobility doctrine), cutting off the armed forces from their primary, central source of fuel and energy supply. The cost of inaction in modernization is no longer merely economic losses, but a real risk of destabilizing the country's defense system and losing operational capabilities on the eastern flank.

2.2. Energy Transition and Hydrogen Logistics Hub

The justification for the immediate expansion of the transport system acquires a modern technological dimension. Płock, as the heart of the Mazovian Hydrogen Valley, has already launched hydrogen purification facilities and is becoming the main distribution hub of green hydrogen for heavy transport in Poland. The transport of hydrogen in high-pressure tube-trailers (350–500 bar) presents massive challenges in terms of counter-terrorism prevention and emergency response. Conducting such transport via old national roads (DK60/DK62) generates a severe risk of accidents and sabotage. Safe, grade-separated infrastructure isolated from cities is essential for distributing this next-generation fuel to strategic consumers.

2.3. Risk Analysis and the Cost of Inaction

Maintaining the current transport anachronism around critical infrastructure generates three fundamental risks:
• Military and Sabotage Threat: The greatest risk is not the physical destruction of installations by missiles, but the logistical "starvation" of the refinery. If an adversary blocks the old, single-carriageway DK60 road or the single-track railway line with a single disabled vehicle, the entire supply and distribution of fuels for the military is paralyzed.
• Lost Economic Opportunities: High-tech industries, such as the IT sector (including modern data centers), the chemical industry, and the hydrogen sector, seek locations with a stable, zero-emission power source like the one Płock offers. These companies forgo investing in the vicinity of Płock solely due to the transport barrier, resulting in a massive loss for the national economy.
• Destructive Financial and Planning Consequences: Permanently postponing crucial investments in the express road network and double-track railway lines generates a skyrocketing increase in the costs of their future implementation. The lack of definitive, central decisions regarding the reservation of transport corridors results in highly inconsistent spatial policies within the Płock agglomeration and in adjacent municipalities. In zones where the state's strategic logistical bypasses should run, chaotic suburbanization and commercial-residential development are progressing. Consequently, every year of delay irreversibly shrinks land reserves, dooms the region to urban paralysis, and will force the State Treasury to pay exponentially higher expropriation compensation when construction inevitably begins, while generating massive and justified social protests. Decision-makers must categorically realize that the postulated transport infrastructure, along with the dynamic technological and production growth of industry in Płock, will have to be built in the future regardless. Attempting to ignore this fact will not halt freight volume growth; it will merely drastically inflate the final cost of investments that the Polish state will eventually be forced to implement under the pressure of a capacity crisis.

2.4. Multimodal Hub in Central Poland

Płock features a unique geographical location – it lies in close proximity to the country's central road and rail hub, Port Polska in Baranów, and the Warsaw and Łódź agglomerations. Its location on the Vistula River, part of the E40 international waterway, creates ideal conditions for building a modern, intermodal Multimodal Hub in Płock. This investment will take the form of an advanced river-rail-road port, located directly on the Włocławek Reservoir. The new complex will integrate the mass cargo flows and hydrogen logistics of strategic state-owned enterprises (ORLEN, PERN), including a dedicated storage zone for containerized and break-bulk cargo, though with the deliberate exclusion of long-term liquid fuel storage installations.
This perspective gains critical importance in the context of the government's Port Polska program, which includes the construction of the central hub in Baranów (located approximately 80 km from Płock) and the deep expansion of the Modlin airport. The postulated Płock Multimodal Hub assumes the status of a strategic, bi-directional consolidation hub. It provides direct passenger-charter integration with the Warsaw-Modlin Airport via the projected LK 247 line, while opening a grade-separated corridor for mass freight flows directed via the Freight Bypass of Płock to the south, straight to the main logistics hub in Baranów. Furthermore, the high hydrological parameters of the Włocławek Reservoir guarantee a significantly extended navigation season and stable draft depths for heavy freight, allowing it to pre-emptively absorb logistical pressure and drastically reduce exhaust emissions from road transport in the spirit of modern climate policy.
The Multimodal Hub will be integrated with a new double-track and electrified access route from the Freight Bypass of Płock, equipped with a grade-separated, double-track switch junction at the connection to the trunk line. The project includes the implementation of an ecological marshalling and maneuvering yard with a track length of 750 meters, featuring a layout of 8 station tracks fully electrified via an automated retractable catenary system, supplemented by a dedicated, electrified 4-track maneuvering bundle for reversing train sets. All loading tracks are served by automated, electric RMG gantry cranes. The green infrastructure includes a cargo pier adapted to class IV waterway parameters with a complex of 3 railway tracks embedded in a paved surface, also covered by the retractable catenary system, and an independent road pier with enhanced structural load capacity, dedicated to heavy road transport, oversized vehicles, and out-of-gauge cargo, including process apparatus and components for the ORLEN production complex, as well as dangerous goods (classified in accordance with ADR and RID agreements).
The projected hub will ultimately replace and expand the functionality of the existing temporary transshipment infrastructure (including the Petrochemia Wharf), transforming a temporary offloading point for large-scale apparatus into a permanent, integrated transport hub of national and international significance, meeting class IV waterway requirements (Figure 1).

2.5. Geopolitical Risk Asymmetry and Requirement for Multi-Area Emergency Support

The Płock agglomeration is characterized by a unique structural specificity in Europe. In contrast to Western European countries, where refining and transmission infrastructure is highly decentralized and geographically dispersed, Poland's energy security system – shaped in a bygone geopolitical era – exhibits an extremely high percentage dependence on a single industrial center. The concentration of the dominant volume of national fuel production in the Płock refinery, combined with its function as an international hub for the PERN pipeline networks, creates a phenomenon of critical risk asymmetry.
In the face of this defined threat structure, a key element of the state's defensive and preventive strategy must be to ensure the highest capacity of transport corridors and a radical reduction in travel time to Płock for external emergency forces. The scale of a potential incident involving a major industrial accident, an act of sabotage, or a hybrid attack in the region of such a massive production complex by definition exceeds the autonomous response capabilities of the region. Emergency, relief, and preventive operations will require the immediate, mass deployment of specialized technical, medical, and firefighting formations from many distant regions of the country.
Consequently, the current collapse of road and rail infrastructure around Płock ceases to be a local problem. It is a critical bottleneck that, under crisis conditions, paralyzes not only the central feedstock and energy hub, but directly destabilizes the feedstock security of the entire Republic of Poland, the refining industry of parts of Germany, and – fundamentally in the context of contemporary asymmetric threats – the operational and logistical capabilities of the entire eastern flank of NATO.
It is worth emphasizing categorically that planned investments in Allied NATO transmission infrastructure (fuel pipeline systems on the eastern flank) will not alleviate these operational risks. Military pipelines, due to their unidirectional supply specificity and assortment constraints, constitute only a component of the logistics system, whose physical origin and production point remains the Płock feedstock and energy hub. Furthermore, under full-scale conflict or asymmetric warfare conditions, underground transmission lines are highly vulnerable to acts of sabotage and cyberattacks on control infrastructure (pumping stations). In accordance with NATO's logistical diversification doctrine, if a pipeline is compromised, the burden of immediate military supply must be absorbed by mass road transport (ADR technical corridors) and rail transport (RID train sets). Without the radical expansion of the S60 Express Road and the construction of a second track from the Trzepowo junction, the country's defense system deprives itself of a logistical bypass, leaving the Alliance's eastern flank without real security in the event of underground network immobilization.
The projected bridge crossings in the Płock region (Rydzyno and Maszewo) are deliberately designed to meet the highest, extraordinary military load classification MLC 150 in accordance with STANAG 2021. This will not only allow for the secure, parallel, and bi-directional deployment of columns of NATO's heaviest tracked vehicles (simultaneous MLC 70/100 movement on both carriageways), but will also fully secure dual-use logistics for transporting critical oversized and non-standard cargo for the ORLEN refining and petrochemical complex.

2.6. Płock as a Multi-Domain Geostrategic Keystone (Center of Gravity): Justification for Crossing Redundancy

Płock cannot be analyzed solely through the lens of standard urban or demographic criteria applied to other cities. In geo-economic and military terms, this center functions as a unique gravitational center for the fuel and energy security of the Republic and the entire eastern flank of NATO. The accumulation within a single geographical space of the largest refining and petrochemical complex (ORLEN) in the region, key crude and petroleum product transmission trunk lines of PERN, and planned hydrogen logistics hubs makes Płock a key critical point of the state's resilience system.
The Vistula River fits into this unique characteristic, playing a multi-dimensional role in the Płock region. On one hand, it constitutes an integral component of the E40 International Waterway, meaning a natural inland logistics highway with immense commercial potential. On the other hand – in a purely defensive and operational dimension – the river is a formidable hydrographic barrier dividing the same geopolitical space into two distinct banks. This dual nature of the Vistula generates an absolute engineering requirement: connecting both banks of the river in a manner that guarantees the continuity of state functions under extreme crisis conditions.
For this reason, the postulated construction of a network of fully dual-carriageway national and city roads, alongside double-track, electrified railway lines, does not stem from local transport needs, but from the doctrine of strategic redundancy. Single lanes or single-track routes represent an easy target for diversionary, hybrid, or kinetic actions, creating critical bottlenecks (single points of failure). A dual-carriageway (2x2) cross-section on the bridges in Maszewo and Rydzyno, combined with a double-track standard for railway crossings on the Freight Bypass of Płock and the new passenger-emergency route, is the only way to physically isolate freight flows, secure civilian evacuation routes, and simultaneously maintain full, uncompromising mobility for NATO forces.
The importance of the Płock industrial hub obliges government administration bodies to completely abandon an archaic, siloed approach to spatial and ministerial planning. All analyses, designs, and their subsequent implementation must be conducted in a fully integrated manner, combining the competencies of ministries responsible for infrastructure, state assets, and defense. These investments require an uncompromising engineering approach – they cannot be the result of halfway budgetary compromises. Their overriding goal is to protect civilian life and health, as well as to guarantee operational continuity for the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland and Allied troops, for whom the Płock hub constitutes a critical, central logistical rear base.

3. ROAD INFRASTRUCTURE: EVACUATION SYSTEMS AND THE ROLE OF THE S60 EXPRESS ROAD


3.1. Elimination of the "Single Point of Failure" (SPoF)

The current road network highly centralizes traffic at the Łódź Północ (Stryków) junction and on the section of the A2 autostrada toward Warsaw. In the coming years, this region will be critically burdened by a skyrocketing growth in transport flows generated by the construction and commissioning of the Port Polska mega-hub in Baranów. Currently pushed plans to routinely widen existing autostradas contradict elementary engineering and planning knowledge. International experience clearly proves that increasing the number of lanes on congested routes does not lead to permanent relief, but instead triggers the phenomenon of induced traffic, which ultimately amplifies transport congestion further.
Under crisis conditions, the paralysis of the Stryków junction freezes transport across central Poland. The postulated S60 route (Kalisz – Płock – Zambrów) serves as a key remedial element in this system (Figure 2). It creates a geometric alternative and a powerful, independent strategic corridor that diverts international and airport transit away from the Warsaw – Łódź axis before it reaches the congested center, effectively dispersing operational risk.
The implementation of the Łódź Południe junction and the S12 and S8 routes on the southern outskirts of Łódź – while fully justified for serving other macro-regions – does not resolve the structural problems of central Poland in relation to northern Mazovia, as these investments in many dimensions still operate within the highly congested Łódź – Warsaw and Silesia – Warsaw routes. In the face of the launch of the Port Polska mega-hub in Baranów, this will lead to a catastrophic accumulation of transport gridlocks on existing access routes. Only the S60 Express Road, as a completely independent, northern horizontal corridor, allows for the physical extraction of mass transit outside this critical area, guaranteeing real dispersion of strategic and defense risk for the state and relieving the central network before it reaches critical points.
The priority section of the express road postulated for Płock on the Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) section constitutes an integral element of the proposed macro-regional transport corridor along the Kalisz – Płock – Zambrów axis (in accordance with the alignment illustrated in Figure 2). Fully breaking the transport peripherality of the Płock region and geometrically increasing the road network resilience of central Poland is strictly dependent on the continuity of this route on a national scale. It is indicated that without the implementation of the remaining sections of this route (Kalisz – Koło and Glinojeck – Zambrów), the Płock section will lose its intended transit and transversal character, generating a risk of local traffic surges. It must simultaneously be reserved that the preparation and implementation of sections located outside the functional area of the city of Płock lies within the exclusive competence of government administration organs and the General Directorate for National Roads and Motorways (GDDKiA), and this study defines them as a crucial and indispensable external condition.

3.2. Connection via 2x2 Roads with the A1 and A2 Autostradas and the Strategic Role of the Glinojeck Junction

Płock currently remains one of the largest and most important industrial centers in Central Europe that is completely cut off from the express road network (autostradas and express roads). This situation generates massive logistical and environmental costs, forcing the transit of thousands of heavy transport rigs and tankers carrying dangerous substances (ADR) through conflicting, single-carriageway national and regional roads (including DK60, DK62).
The construction of the S60 Express Road on the Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) section in a dual-carriageway (2x2) standard, along with a new dual-carriageway (2x2) bridge over the Vistula at the height of Rydzyno near Płock, represents a key element in eliminating this structural anomaly. The implementation of this corridor provides a triple logistical-defense synergy:
• Direct Connection to the North-South Axis (Glinojeck / S7 junction): Connecting Płock with the Glinojeck junction on the S7 express road opens immediate, grade-separated access to main national arteries. In the northern direction, this ensures smooth and secure transport of refined and petrochemical products to the seaports in Gdańsk and Gdynia. In the south-eastern direction, it guarantees a fast connection to the Warsaw agglomeration.
• Western and Southern Distribution (Kutno / Koło / A1 and A2): Routing the S60 route south-west toward Koło (via integrated links along the Kutno – Gostynin axis) allows for direct and grade-separated connection of the Płock hub with the A2 autostrada (Łódź – Poznań – Berlin) and the A1 autostrada (Gdańsk/Ports – Łódź – Silesia). This is a crucial solution for mass freight transport, relieving the existing, congested network of national and regional roads and drastically shortening delivery times for feedstocks and finished products.
• Northern Logistics-Transport Corridors and Network Resilience: Thanks to the S60 route, a network of alternative logistics-transport corridors will be established around Płock. In the event of a blockade, breakdown, or acts of sabotage on one of the key arteries of central Poland, the Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) section allows for the immediate redirection of strategic flows of cargo and chemical tankers via the newly created lanes of the express road. This prevents a logistical and operational paralysis of the entire region. From the perspective of transport engineering and crisis management, this section is not merely an investment of a local or regional character. It constitutes a crucial link connecting the autostrada network of western Poland with the key S7 express corridor, creating a new, secure, and highly efficient transversal transport axis for the state. The logistical structure of the Płock agglomeration in terms of security and transport must rely on four independent, mutually reinforcing road systems:
• City Ring Road as an Internal Emergency-Evacuation Bypass (2x2): A high-priority task within the city's urban structure is to consolidate existing dual-carriageway (2x2) sections into an uninterrupted class GP sequence, creating a complete internal bypass for Płock. In the event of a major industrial accident, a sabotage attack, or a hybrid threat, the wide profile of this artery ensures critical capacity. It guarantees safe, mass evacuation of the civilian population from danger zones, while leaving a clear, secure lane for the efficient influx of external emergency forces, medical formations, and deploying military columns. The key element closing this strategic layout will be the construction of a new, dual-carriageway (2x2) bridge over the Vistula at the height of Maszewo (military load class MLC 150 / STANAG, ADR segregation), which will ensure evacuation corridor continuity and an isolated route for transporting dangerous goods (ADR) exiting the ORLEN complex.
• Internal City Emergency-Evacuation Corridor on the East-West Axis (2x2): Within Płock's urban structure, a key task is to fully close and consolidate existing dual-carriageway (2x2) sections of city arteries on the east-west axis into a single spójny and uninterrupted class GP and G transport corridor. This requires constructing a second carriageway along Ignacego Łukasiewicza Street, along with implementing a second, parallel span of the Płock June 1976 Bridge featuring two traffic lanes across the Brzeźnica River. Under critical conditions – such as a technological disaster, an incident of a diversionary character, or an escalation of hybrid actions – this artery will assume the function of the primary vector for outward vehicle flows from heavily urbanized zones of Płock to secure relocation areas. The architecture of this route eliminates the risk of transport paralysis and gridlocks, enabling grade-separated dispersion of the civilian population. Simultaneously, maintaining a dedicated lane remains a tactical priority, indispensable for the rapid entry of support from the outside: emergency teams, medical convoys, and deploying components of the Polish Armed Forces.
• Access Roads Connecting the City Ring Road with High-Speed Routes (S10 and S60) in a 2x2 Standard: The essence of this layout lies in the dual-carriageway (2x2) class GP access roads radiating from the City Ring Road, directing city, freight, evacuation-emergency, and operational traffic for the armed forces directly to external junctions on express roads and the national network in all directions.
• Local logistics-transport corridors for ADR transport and industrial infrastructure: A completely separate function in the security system is performed by a network of single-carriageway logistics-transport roads located on the northern side of the ORLEN production complex. These routes, radiating directly from the factory gates in various directions toward external junctions of high-speed roads, constitute specialized freight arteries. Their sole task is to immediately capture and safely route flows of dangerous goods (ADR) from refinery lands onto the network of national and express roads. Isolating this specific, heaviest traffic on the northern side of the plant allows for its complete decoupling from the city's internal residential zones and eliminates any risk of conflict with civilian evacuation traffic. This system operates in full synergy with a new, dedicated section of a single-carriageway logistics-transport road approximately 1.5 kilometers in length, located on the south-western side of the plant, constituting an alternative alignment on the section between the transshipment terminal on the Vistula and the ORLEN production plant, necessitated by postulated transport solutions in this area of the city. This section, constituting an engineered corridor with extreme subgrade load capacity, will connect the Vistula bank (the Petrochemia Wharf transshipment terminal) directly with the factory gates, securing an un-alternative and isolated route for overland transport of oversized and non-standard cargo.

3.3. Overcoming the Vistula Barrier in Płock and Countering Planners' Criticisms

The right-bank, industrial Płock is cut off from the left bank of the river by the Vistula, which in light of threats from Russia necessitates the construction of new, fortified crossings precisely here, rather than dozens of kilometers away. Government planners advance the criticism that drawing the express road close to Płock creates a "co-located target" (refinery + junction) for an enemy attack.
However, this criticism relies on obsolete planning assumptions. The refining and petrochemical complex in Płock is already a top-priority strategic target. The lack of modern transport infrastructure in its immediate vicinity does not reduce attack risks; it merely drastically lowers the country's defensive, emergency response, and logistical capabilities in a crisis situation. The new crossing over the Vistula and the S60 route in the Płock region do not generate additional threats – they disperse that threat, eliminating the existing, critical transport bottleneck and giving the Polish Armed Forces and emergency services indispensable operational flexibility. Such a line of thinking is an error from the perspective of NATO defensive tactics:
• The location of the ORLEN industrial complex in Płock is perfectly known via satellite: narrow roads will not hide the plant from adversary reconnaissance.
• An express road (2x2) provides flexibility: after damage to one lane, traffic can still proceed via the emergency lane or the adjacent carriageway.
• A wide route outside urbanized terrain: creates ideal maneuvering positions for mobile air and missile defense systems (e.g., Patriot, Mała Narew), which must protect the skies over the refinery.
• Countering asymmetric threats (drones): experiences from new-generation conflicts prove that dense urban development impedes the detection and engagement of unmanned aerial vehicles (radar errors, shrapnel risks). Open corridors of express routes and the City Ring Road provide an excellent operating base for mobile anti-drone systems (C-UAS) and electronic warfare (EW) systems, which can operate freely on the refinery's flanks and dynamically alter positions.
Consequently, the construction of two dual-carriageway road crossings over the Vistula is necessary:
• A bridge in a dual-carriageway (2x2) standard over the Vistula within the City Ring Road at the height of Maszewo: The bridge, compliant with military load class MLC 150 / STANAG for ADR transport, will secure a dedicated evacuation route and an isolated corridor for transporting dangerous goods (ADR) from the ORLEN complex.
• A dual-carriageway (2x2) express bridge over the Vistula within the S60 road at the height of Rydzyno near Płock: The new, fortified crossing with the highest military-engineering parameters (STANAG class 150, MLC 70/100 load), located outside the center, will form a "lifeline" for rapid reaction forces directed toward the refinery from the south-western direction, bypassing Płock from the east and heading toward the north-eastern borders of the country.

3.4. Alignment through the Gostynin-Włocławek Landscape Park

The construction of the postulated S60 route along the optimal, grade-separated alignment necessitates traversing a peripheral fragment of the Gostynin-Włocławek Landscape Park. Implementing this solution is entirely devoid of real spatial alternatives. It represents an absolute priority for ensuring the efficient evacuation of the population from the left-bank (southern) and remaining districts of Płock, guaranteeing direct access to a high-capacity trunk line that funnels vehicle flows out of the potential hazard area.
Simultaneously, this alignment dictates uncompromising military mobility, enabling – in sudden, crisis situations and under conditions of immediate hybrid threats – rapid and protected transport of armed forces from the left bank of the Vistula directly into the region of the strategic refining and petrochemical complex.

3.4.1. Strategy for Minimizing the Ecological Footprint and Environmental Compensation in the GWLP Corridor

Awareness of the S60 route traversing a peripheral fragment of the Gostynin-Włocławek Landscape Park imposes an obligation on designers to apply above-standard biosphere protection measures. To prevent the postulated defense corridor from becoming a barrier to animal migration corridors, the technical design must rely on the "green tunnel" doctrine.
Within the framework of the investment, the following are envisioned:
• The construction of large-scale overpasses for animals (ecoducts) with a minimum width of 60 meters, located at the sites of traditional migration routes of large mammals (cervids, wild boars).
• The utilization of viaducts instead of classic embankments on key, waterlogged valley sections, which will preserve full hydrological continuity and the free flow of groundwater and surface waters.
• The installation of 24-hour guidance systems featuring low-intensity lighting, alongside noise and anti-glare screens with enhanced dampening parameters (next-generation absorbing panels), minimizing habitat fragmentation for birds and bats.
As part of environmental compensation (in accordance with Article 75 of the Environmental Protection Act), it is postulated that the investor finance a restorative afforestation program on lands adjacent to the park over an area three times larger than the terrain occupied by road construction, as well as modernize the tourist-educational infrastructure of the GWLP. Thanks to this, the realization of the military and emergency objective will be integrated with enhancing the ecological resilience of the entire forest complex.

4. RAILWAY INFRASTRUCTURE: FREIGHT BYPASS AND PASSENGER TRUNK HUB


4.1. Freight Bypass of Płock and Rejection of the Suburban Variant

Meeting public safety requirements and SEVESO III regulations necessitates the immediate extraction of heavy freight trains carrying dangerous substances (in accordance with regulations) from the city's densely populated residential zone to its outskirts.
• Criticism of the Variant through the Stara Biała Municipality: The previously proposed route through Maszewo Duże, Mańkowo, Wyszyna, and Stara Biała drastically damages a rapidly expanding, suburban zone of the agglomeration. This area serves as a functional urban rear base. Introducing heavy chemical train movements there will trigger urban chaos and devastate investment opportunities for a region that is already losing residents to the suburban zone.
• Compromise Variant: The freight rail route should be aligned through sparsely populated areas directly along the western administrative boundary of the city, in the region of Maszewo and the Winiary district.
• The geometry of the western variant of the Freight Bypass of Płock, assuming its alignment at an optimal distance of approximately 500 meters from the western boundary of the ORLEN production complex, represents a well-considered spatial and security compromise. This location, on one hand, protects the dynamically developing suburban zone from urban chaos, and on the other, creates a critical security buffer preventing so-called cascading failures (domino effects). Contemporary engineering standards in Western European chemical hubs rely on an island (modular) structure – strategic transport infrastructure must share space with the industrial complex, serving as its direct logistical and emergency rear base rather than a developmental barrier. This distance guarantees the full operational continuity of the refinery under crisis conditions, while leaving the indispensable land reserve for projected western structural investments of the corporation.

4.2. Trunk Link and New Railway Crossings

The new railway layout of the agglomeration must be integrated into the national and international network through:
• The construction of a new double-track railway bridge in the western districts of Płock along the Freight Bypass of Płock: The alignment of the new bridge crossing is characterized by an increased longitudinal gradient, stemming directly from the geomorphological asymmetry of the Vistula valley and the necessity to overcome a significant elevation difference between its banks (the high Płock Escarpment and the low-lying flood terrace of the left bank). In terms of railway traffic engineering, this feature does not constitute an operational barrier. The longitudinal profile of the route exhibits full synergy with the primary freight flow: the track gradient favors the direction of outward transport of mass, fully loaded RID tanker sets from the refinery to the south of the country. Conversely, lighter return trains (with empty wagons) will overcome the projected incline without difficulty, fully balancing the energetic and operational characteristics of the line. This effect is further amplified by rapid technological progress in the rolling stock industry; contemporary six-axle freight locomotives equipped with digital anti-slip systems and the option to employ multiple traction entirely eliminate former operational constraints on elevated longitudinal profiles. Crucially in a social-spatial dimension, this specific western variant of bridge and Freight Bypass routing eliminates key localization conflicts. In contrast to previous, flawed concepts, the new line completely bypasses and does not bisect dense settlement structures and villages in the Stara Biała municipality. This solution permanently protects the territorial integrity of adjacent communities, drastically reduces eventual expropriation costs, and eliminates the risk of social protests that could block this investment, which is critical for state security, for decades.
• The construction of a second track and the electrification of LK 33 on the Kutno – Trzepowo section (terminating in the vicinity of the planned junction with the CMK-North trunk line in Trzepowo – at the connection point of the postulated double-track link to LK 33, integrating this line with the Freight Bypass of Płock, as illustrated in Figure 1) with a new, double-track railway crossing over the Vistula in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge, which requires shifting LK 33 to a new alignment approximately 10 kilometers in length along with the bridge crossing itself. The implementation of this strategic corridor on the left bank of the Vistula is directly linked to constructing the new Płock Radziwie station in a new location inside the railway ring structure, as well as launching the new Płock Góry station, serving as a key passenger node on the approach to the hub from the Kutno direction. The new crossing is intended primarily to serve passenger traffic, creating a grade-separated and efficient passenger corridor that will relieve the central part of the city. This modern bridge will be a crucial infrastructural element enabling the smooth operation of trains turning south in Płock from the newly planned CMK-North trunk line within the north-south core corridor, as included in the government document Integrated Railway Network (ZSK), and the main Płock railway station will consequently absorb leading passenger flows. Simultaneously, the new railway bridge will play a strategic role as an emergency crossing for heavy freight traffic from the ORLEN refinery and NATO military transports. In a crisis situation or in the event of an outage on other sections, it will ensure indispensable double-track logistical continuity and connect the left-bank Multimodal Hub with the rest of the country. The currently operated Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge possesses critical technical limitations as a single-track structure. Leaving it as the sole passenger crossing in the face of the planned launch of the new Płock – Ciechanów – Przasnysz railway line and the aforementioned flows from CMK-North will lead to complete transport inadequacy of the system, and further overloading of the old facility carries a real risk of structural failure. Both mass flows of heavy freight trains from the ORLEN production plant and passenger trains operated on the north-south axis from CMK-North, LK 33 from the northern direction, and the Płock – Ciechanów – Przasnysz line southward toward Kutno absolutely require increased capacity through the implementation of the postulated second track on LK 33 on the Kutno – Trzepowo section. The ultimate stage of this modernization – precisely illustrated on the attached map (Figure 1) – will be the complete liquidation and dismantling of the obsolete section of LK 33 running across the century-old Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge, allowing for the definitive removal of heavy train traffic from Płock's strict downtown zone and relieving this worn engineering structure.
• The construction of a double-track railway link connecting the new Freight Bypass of Płock directly with the CMK-North trunk line: This is a fully double-track link with bilateral, grade-separated switch junctions. This solution is crucial for the efficient outward transport of mass RID shipments from the ORLEN refinery, the execution of freight transport on the CMK-North – LK 33 axis in the southern direction, and the operation of the postulated Multimodal Hub. Supplementing this layout is the construction of smaller links that will integrate the new bypass infrastructure with the existing LK 33 (via a double-track connection) and with the newly designed line to Przasnysz via a single-track link routed further from LK 33. This will unlock full capacity for broad cargo flows in the region while completely bypassing Płock's urbanized center. Meanwhile, on the left bank of the Vistula, on the border of the Płock districts of Radziwie and Góry, the implementation of a grade-separated triangular junction connecting the Freight Bypass of Płock with the new alignment section of LK 33 will ensure full flexibility for passenger and emergency relations and allow for closing the railway ring around Płock, opening the possibility to establish the Płock Urban Fast Rail (SKM).
• The Płock Urban Fast Rail (SKM) Project: The proposed track layout, connecting the western Freight Bypass of Płock with the new double-track crossing over the Vistula in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge and the existing LK 33, creates a unique opportunity to close a complete railway ring around the agglomeration. This geometry allows for launching an ecological, zero-emission Urban Fast Rail (SKM) operating on a closed loop around the city. In an agglomeration with an extraordinary, elevated industrial risk, rail transport independent of the road network represents a fundamental element of civil security – it serves as a strategic evacuation backbone for the population, while securing efficient, daily passenger communication in the spirit of transport decarbonization policies. An absolute condition for the efficiency and safety of a system so constructed is to ensure full, grade-separated isolation of railway routes from the road network across the entire territory of Płock. The construction of modern multi-level crossings (flyovers and tunnels) is crucial for eliminating the risk of paralyzing city arteries given a high frequency of passenger train operations and guarantees that railway crossings will not block emergency and evacuation routes in zones covered by SEVESO III directive requirements (Figure 1).

6. INVESTMENT PHASING STRATEGY AND IMPLEMENTATION SCHEDULE

Taking into account the unprecedented scale of the integrated infrastructural package, implementing all facilities within a single time window is impossible from the standpoint of budgetary limits and construction market execution potential. To ensure financial and operational continuity, the investment must be divided into three autonomous, logically linked phases with diversified financing sources:

Phase I: Critical Priority (Years 2027–2030) – Bottleneck Elimination

• Roads: Commencement of design work (STEŚ) for the S60 Express Road and construction of Płock's internal City Ring Road along with a dual-carriageway (2x2) bridge in Maszewo (MLC 150 / STANAG load capacity, ADR segregation) and closing the internal city emergency-evacuation corridor on the east-west axis. Financing: CEF Transport – Military Mobility program (up to 50% EU co-financing) + Ministry of National Defense budget (Armed Forces Support Fund).

Phase II: Strategic Integration (Years 2030–2034) – Overcoming the Vistula Barrier and Connecting to the National Network

• Roads: Construction of the high-priority and strategic section of the S60 Express Road in the Koło junction (A2) – junction in the region of Lubień Kujawski (A1) – Gostynin – Płock – Glinojeck junction (S7) corridor in a dual-carriageway (2x2) standard, along with a new dual-carriageway (2x2) express bridge over the Vistula at the height of Rydzyno near Płock with the highest military-engineering parameters (MLC 150 / STANAG load capacity). This section will break the transport isolation of the city, plugging the Płock industrial hub directly into the A1/A2 autostrada network and the S7 express road, as well as finalizing access road construction on the City Ring Road – express roads route and logistics-transport corridors in the region of the ORLEN industrial complex. Financing: Government Program for National Road Construction (RPBDK).
• Rail: Construction of a second track and electrification of the LK 33 section on the Kutno – Trzepowo route, combined with constructing a new alignment of the LK 33 section at the height of the Solidarność Bridge as a dedicated passenger-emergency route along with a new, double-track railway bridge over the Vistula and a new location for the Płock Radziwie railway station, plus a grade-separated triangular junction on the border of Radziwie and Góry, ensuring full flexibility for passenger and emergency relations. Financing: National Railway Program (KPK) + PKP PLK internal funds.

Phase III: Full Synergy and Decarbonization (Years 2034–2037) – Multimodal Ring

• Rail: Launch of the Płock Urban Fast Rail (SKM) on a closed loop around the agglomeration, construction of multi-level grade-separated crossings at intersections of the railway ring with city road arteries, construction of links integrating the Freight Bypass of Płock with the CMK-North line and LK 33 with the line to Przasnysz, and the final dismantling of the obsolete section of LK 33 from the Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge. Financing: European Funds for Infrastructure, Climate, Environment (FEnIKS).
• Multimodal Hub: Construction of a river-rail-road port with transshipment terminals and a storage zone on the Włocławek Reservoir, integrating mass cargo flows with Port Polska in Baranów, with the deliberate exclusion of long-term liquid fuel storage installations. Financing: Public-Private Partnership (PPP) initiated by public entities – strategic state-owned enterprises (ORLEN, PERN) in cooperation with commercial private partners and supported by EU funds.
This division guarantees that each completed stage immediately delivers real logistical and defensive utility, without waiting for the finalization of the entire program.

8. IMPLEMENTATION RISK MATRIX AND MANAGEMENT STRATEGY

Identifying risks at the conceptual stage allows for the preventive implementation of legal and engineering procedures, preventing implementation delays.
• Environmental Risk (Crossing the GWLP and Vistula Valley)
	• Threat: Protests by environmental organizations, protracted procedures for obtaining environmental impact assessment decisions (DUŚ), risk of blocking S60 road and bridge construction by the General Directorate for Environmental Protection.
	• Mitigation: Implementation of the "green tunnel" doctrine (subparagraph 3.4.1), close cooperation with the GWLP Directorate at the design stage, utilizing viaducts instead of classic embankments in waterlogged regions, and proposing a threefold spatial afforestation compensation.
• Risk of Conflict with Underground Infrastructure (PERN and ORLEN Pipelines)
	• Threat: Damage to underground crude, product, or gas trunk lines during earthworks, operational paralysis of the refinery, environmental disaster.
	• Mitigation: Application of the legal requirement for spatial flexibility (subsection 2.1). Utilization of trenchless horizontal directional drilling (HDD) technologies and microtunneling at intersections of road and rail routes with existing feedstock corridors. Work executed under the technical supervision of critical infrastructure operators.
• Geological-Engineering Risk (Asymmetry of Banks and the Płock Escarpment)
	• Threat: Earth landslides, loss of slope stability, soil liquefaction, and subgrade instability in the construction zone of new road and rail bridge abutments, resulting from a major elevation difference (approximately 40-50 meters) between the high right bank (Płock Escarpment) and the low, flood-prone left bank of the Vistula.
	• Mitigation: Execution of deep and dense geotechnical borings and static cone penetration tests (CPTu) prior to selecting the final foundation technology for engineering object piers. Utilization of deep foundations on large-diameter piles (bored in casing pipes) and slurry walls anchored in the valley floor. Implementation of an automated system for continuous structural and geodetic monitoring, utilizing fiber-optic sensors, inclinometers, and robotic total stations, to permanently test the slope stability of the Płock Escarpment during both the construction phase and multi-year bridge operation.
• Social-Expropriation Risk (Protests by Residents of Suburban Zones)
	• Threat: Administrative blockades, social protests against bisecting settlement structures with heavy RID/ADR transport, lengthening of compensation procedures.
	• Mitigation: Categorical rejection of the railway variant through the Stara Biała municipality (Maszewo Duże, Mańkowo). Selection of the recommended western variant (subsection 4.1), running through sparsely populated areas within the industrial buffer zone. Utilization of special-purpose act procedures (road and rail) while simultaneously securing funds in the state budget for fair and rapid compensation payments according to real market rates increased by a bonus for voluntary property surrender.

9. SYNTHESIS OF CAPACITY AND TRAFFIC FLOWS ACROSS STRATEGIC VISTULA CORRIDORS


9.1. Structural Inadequacy and Functional Asymmetry of Crossings

The current distribution of Annual Average Daily Traffic (AADT) in the Płock hub reveals a critical asymmetry of burdens and functions. In the existing state, the Solidarność Bridge secures the road freight corridor, absorbing the dominant volume of heavy truck transport and dangerous substances (ADR), which stems from its technical parameters and the routing of relations outside dense development. However, this model exhausts its efficiency in the target framework.
Conversely, the Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge remains severely overloaded with passenger, agglomeration, and public transport flows, while performing the critical function of the sole right-bank crossing for railway freight traffic. The necessity to route heavy freight sets and dangerous substances (RID) across the structure of an old, single-track bridge and adjacent residential neighborhoods drastically lowers civil security levels. In a strategic sense, this means the city and regional transport system lacks flexibility – any blockade of one of these crossings immediately paralyzes the entire city structure, cutting off the country's strategic production base in both road and rail dimensions.

9.2. Depletion of Daily Capacity in Evacuation Corridors

Traffic measurements demonstrate that the historical transport corridor based on the Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge regularly reaches critical saturation during peak transport hours, approaching theoretical daily capacity limits. From the standpoint of civil security (crisis management in SEVESO III zones), constant congestion in this area generates unacceptable risks. The existing geometric layout of access roads prevents efficient vehicle distribution, which in the event of an urgent need to evacuate the population from the right-bank, industrial part of Płock will lead to an immediate paralysis of emergency corridors.
An analogous state of critical capacity depletion and traffic flow saturation is recorded in the railway corridor based on the same century-old bridge structure. Accumulating RID freight flows from the refinery and passenger traffic toward Kutno on a single, obsolete, and single-track facility creates a structural logistical gridlock. In the event of hybrid threat materialization or technical failure, the lack of an alternative rail crossing prevents utilizing the railway as a secure, high-capacity evacuation backbone for the civilian population.

9.3. Projected Transport Network Paralysis and Induced Traffic

Current road traffic volumes on national roads DK60 and DK62 in the Płock region exhibit a steady upward trend. Within the strategic horizon to 2036, in light of the launch of the Port Polska mega-hub in Baranów, a drastic accumulation of freight flows along the Warsaw – Łódź axis is projected. Leaving Płock without direct connection to the external network of high-speed routes will cause an influx of international flows of heavy vehicles directly onto the city's internal arteries, generating a permanent logistical gridlock and preventing the timely distribution of fuels and green hydrogen within the country's defense structure.
Parallelly, a surging increase in capacity demand will impact railway routes. Introducing the new CMK-North trunk line and the planned line to Przasnysz will trigger the phenomenon of induced railway traffic. Failing to segregate feedstock logistics flows from newly created, high-speed passenger corridors will lead to complete operational inadequacy of the Płock hub. Under crisis conditions, this will prevent the smooth operation of passenger trains and drastically lengthen response times for NATO military transports (the military mobility doctrine).

9.4. Conclusions and Recommendations for the Infrastructural Masterplan

• Necessity to Construct New Diversification Corridors: Traffic flow analysis results unconditionally justify rejecting the concept of routinely modernizing the existing road and track network. The sole strategic solution eliminating the "single point of failure" (SPoF) phenomenon is implementing new, independent crossings over the Vistula:
	• In the Road Component: An express crossing within the postulated S60 road (in the Rydzyno region) and an orbital crossing within the City Ring Road (in the Maszewo region).
	• In the Railway Component: A freight-military crossing within the Freight Bypass of Płock (in the Maszewo region) and a passenger-emergency crossing (in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge).
• Physical Separation of Transport Flows and Protection of Evacuation Corridors: Implementing a full, three-level isolation of road traffic is recommended. A key action is relocating international and main national flows of ADR logistics and heavy freight to Płock's external bypass within the S60 Express Road (via the postulated bridge in Rydzyno). Meanwhile, a portion of intra-regional and industrial transport will be directed to the western city bypass, which comprises a dual-carriageway access road on the section from the junction with the S10 route in Proboszczewice to the western areas of the ORLEN industrial complex, followed by the City Ring Road on the section from the corporation's production plant to the road bridge over the Vistula in the Maszewo region and onward to the left bank of the Vistula. Nearly all mentioned transport traffic flows, moving between the refining and petrochemical complex and high-speed roads, will be executed via dedicated logistics-transport corridors for dangerous goods (ADR) and non-standard cargo. Applying the presented solutions will allow for relieving the internal street network and the Solidarność Bridge for local economic distribution needs, and will guarantee absolute patency and security for the city's emergency-evacuation corridors in crisis situations.
• Elimination of RID Threats and Implementation of the Railway Ring: In the railway component, the absolute priority of the Masterplan is the complete extraction of dangerous goods (RID) transit from residential zones through constructing the western variant of the Freight Bypass of Płock. Relocating passenger traffic to a new, double-track and electrified LK 33 alignment along with a new crossing in the vicinity of the Solidarność Bridge will allow for the definitive dismantling of the obsolete and conflicting track section from the Legiony Józefa Piłsudskiego Bridge. Both mass flows of heavy freight trains from the ORLEN production plant and passenger trains operated on the north-south axis from CMK-North absolutely require increased capacity through the implementation of the postulated second track on LK 33 on the Kutno – Trzepowo section. This geometry will ultimately enable closing a complete railway ring around the agglomeration, which will allow for launching an ecological Urban Fast Rail (SKM) performing the role of an independent, grade-separated trunk evacuation corridor in crisis situations.

SUMMARY

The radical modernization and expansion of Płock's transport system represents a prerequisite for maintaining Poland's fuel and energy security. The new Railway Freight Bypass, the passenger bridge over the Vistula, the City Ring Road, and the postulated S60 Express Road constitute integrated, mutually reinforcing pillars of a modern logistics network. The mentioned infrastructural needs are relatively low in relation to the volume of GDP that the Płock industrial center has generated for decades, currently generates, and will generate in the future.
These investments represent a financial and logistical security policy for the country. The entirety of the assumptions exhibits full compatibility with the EU doctrine of dual-use facilities. This guarantees the absolute eligibility of the project to secure targeted EU funds from the CEF Transport – Military Mobility program, as well as from the national defense budget currently exceeding the level of 4.8% of GDP. Poland's most important energy, fuel, and hydrogen hub must cease to be a transport island.
In conclusion, the integrated implementation of all postulated infrastructural projects – fully justified by Płock's strategic geographical location in the geometric center of the country and over the crucial Vistula corridor – constitutes an indispensable foundation resolving the critical logistical problems of the region. Although implementing this concept involves serious budgetary outlays, in the final macroeconomic calculation it generates a powerful, unprecedented added value. These investments permanently protect the operational continuity of the country's feedstock heart, reconfigure the transport map of central Poland, and elevate the strategic resilience of NATO's eastern flank to the highest level of continental security.
